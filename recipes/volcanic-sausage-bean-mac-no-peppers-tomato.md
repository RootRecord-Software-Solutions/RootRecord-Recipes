# Quiet Eruption: Tomato Sausage Bean Mac

*A pepper-free variation of [Volcanic Sausage Bean Mac](volcanic-sausage-bean-mac.md).*

This one-pot macaroni combines Chinese-style sausage, onion, tomato paste, and pork-and-beans in tomato sauce. It uses no jalapeños or Hawaiian chili peppers, making it a milder alternative to the original.

## Ingredients

- Elbow macaroni
- Onion, chopped
- Chinese-style sausage
- Tomato paste
- Van Camp's Pork & Beans in Tomato Sauce
- Water, for cooking the macaroni
- Reduced-sodium brown gravy mix, optional

**Amounts:** Adjust to your preferred batch size. The original variation did not specify exact macaroni, sausage, onion, or bean quantities; the tomato paste was added by the can, with the can size unspecified. The amount of gravy mix used was not established.

## Instructions

1. Add the macaroni, chopped onion, and whole Chinese-style sausage to a pot of water. Cook the pasta until tender and the sausage according to its package instructions, ensuring it is fully cooked.
2. Remove the sausage, slice into bite-size pieces, and set aside. Drain excess cooking water from the macaroni, reserving some to adjust the sauce.
3. Stir tomato paste into the macaroni and onion mixture. Add reserved cooking water a little at a time until the sauce reaches a consistency you like.
4. Stir in the pork-and-beans in tomato sauce and heat through.
5. **Optional:** If using brown gravy mix, prepare it according to the package directions before incorporating it into the warm mixture.
6. Return the sliced sausage to the pot, combine gently, and serve warm.

## Cooking notes

- **No added hot peppers:** This version intentionally omits the peppers included in the original dish.
- **Bean choice matters:** Use pork-and-beans in tomato sauce, rather than the brown-sugar baked beans in the original recipe.
- **Gravy is optional:** It can be used for a richer sauce, but the exact amount in the original variation was not recorded.
- **Adjust the texture:** A splash of reserved macaroni water helps loosen a sauce that becomes too thick.
